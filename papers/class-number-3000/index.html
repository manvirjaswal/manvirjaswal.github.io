Imaginary quadratic fields of class number at most 3000

Manvir Jaswal

PDFZenodo recordPrograms and data

Abstract

Assume Kimura's explicit bound for real zeros of Dirichlet L-functions (the second part of Theorem 1 of his preprint, Lean-checked according to its author but not refereed), which follows from the hypothesis (H) that L(σ, χ) ≠ 0 for 7/8 < σ < 1 and every real primitive character χ. Then exactly 36 993 853 imaginary quadratic fields have class number at most 3000, and the largest absolute discriminant among them is 2 562 211 723. We also list the imaginary quadratic orders of class number at most 1095, and so the singular moduli of degree at most 1095. Kimura's bound gives h(D) ≥ 3562 for |D| ≥ e^35.0131 by Hecke's integral representation of the Dedekind zeta function, and a sieve in the manner of Watkins, the tabulation of Mosunov and Jacobson and an unconditional computation with binary quadratic forms treat smaller |D|. Hypothesis (H) follows from the generalized Riemann hypothesis and is the real case of the zero-free half-plane Re s > 7/8 announced by OpenAI in October 2026, whose Lean formalization is machine-checked but whose proof has not been refereed. Under (H) the sieve and the tabulation are not needed, h(D) > √|D|/(5 log|D|) for every imaginary quadratic field, and several classifications known only up to one possible exception become complete, among them those of the imaginary quadratic fields with class group of exponent 2, 4 or 8 and of the real quadratic fields of extended Richaud–Degert type with class number 1. Our lists agree with those of Kimura (h ≤ 2000), of Cremona and Sutherland (h ≤ 1000, under the generalized Riemann hypothesis) and of a computation posted on October 8, 2026, in the repository zeta-lab (h ≤ 1500, assuming that L(s, χ_D) ≠ 0 for Re s > 7/8), and with the counts of Garcia, Grenié, Lee and Molteni (h ≤ 2000, under the generalized Riemann hypothesis).
