I'm a software engineer by background, and I built the AI system behind this work. I'm also interested in getting more involved in AI research, building on what I've learned from developing the system. There's more in the FAQ below.

The generalized Fermat equation xp + yp = z5 and relative class numbers

We prove that the equation xp + yp = z5 has no solution in nonzero coprime integers for every prime p ≥ 13 such that p ≢ 1 (mod 5) and 5 ∤ h−(ℚ(ζ5p)), using results of Chen and Koutsianas (to appear in Algebra & Number Theory) on Darmon's Frey surfaces. These conditions hold at 41 primes 13 ≤ p < 1000 and at 2432 primes 13 ≤ p < 105.

Preprint, 2026

PaperPrograms and data
Abstract

We prove that the equation xᵖ + yᵖ = z⁵ has no solution in nonzero coprime integers for every prime p ≥ 13 such that p ≢ 1 (mod 5) and 5 ∤ h⁻(ℚ(ζ₅ₚ)), using results of Chen and Koutsianas (to appear in Algebra & Number Theory) on Darmon's Frey surfaces. These conditions hold at 41 primes 13 ≤ p < 1000 and at 2432 primes 13 ≤ p < 10⁵. For every prime p ≥ 7 with p ≢ 1 (mod 5) and 5 ∤ h⁻(ℚ(ζ₅ₚ)) we prove, without these results, that no solution (a, b, c) has 10p ∣ ab. Such solutions are close, at each of 2, 5 and p, to one of the trivial solutions (1, 0, 1), (0, 1, 1), which survive every local test. To exclude such solutions we attach to a solution a curve of genus 2(p − 1) whose Jacobian has multiplication by ℤ[ζ₅, ζₚ + ζₚ⁻¹]. It belongs to a family of quotients, by an involution, of cyclic covers in a hypergeometric family of the kind used in Darmon's program. The member of this family at the trivial solution is Y⁵ = Rᵖ − 1, which has complex multiplication, and we compare the 5-adic Tate modules of the two curves one power of 1 − ζ₅ at a time. The obstructions to extending this comparison lie in Selmer groups that vanish when 5 ∤ h⁻(ℚ(ζ₅ₚ)), as in Kummer's theorem for regular primes; the local condition at 5 comes from Breuil–Kisin modules over a tamely ramified extension. The generalized Riemann hypothesis is not used.

13
Elliptic curves with torsion group ℤ/6ℤ and generic rank four

We give an elliptic curve over ℚ(t) with non-constant j-invariant, torsion subgroup ℤ/6ℤ and Mordell–Weil rank exactly 4. The largest rank previously known for such a curve was 3, due to Lecacheux and reached by several later families.

Preprint, 2026

PaperPrograms and data
Abstract

We give an elliptic curve over ℚ(t) with non-constant j-invariant, torsion subgroup ℤ/6ℤ and Mordell–Weil rank exactly 4. The largest rank previously known for such a curve was 3, due to Lecacheux and reached by several later families. The curve is the pullback of the universal elliptic curve with a point of order 6 along an explicit rational function of degree 16 that is invariant under t ↦ −t and t ↦ 1/t. The upper bound for the rank comes from the specialization criterion of Gusić and Tadić and a 2-descent of a single fiber. The rank splits along the characters of the symmetry group: the curve descends to ℚ((t + 1/t)²), where it and three of its quadratic twists have rank exactly 1. All but finitely many rational fibers have torsion subgroup ℤ/6ℤ and rank at least 4. We also give an explicit family over an elliptic curve of rank two which shows that infinitely many elliptic curves over ℚ have torsion subgroup ℤ/6ℤ and rank at least 5, a bound that Dujella's tables credit to Eroshkin (2009, personal communication). One member of this family has rank exactly 6. Over ℚ(√2) the same kind of construction gives infinitely many curves with torsion subgroup ℤ/6ℤ and rank at least 6; whether the same holds over ℚ is open. All results are unconditional.

12
Infinitely many elliptic curves with torsion ℤ/2ℤ × ℤ/4ℤ and rank at least six

We show that there are infinitely many elliptic curves over ℚ with torsion subgroup ℤ/2ℤ × ℤ/4ℤ and rank at least 6. This answers the case ℤ/2ℤ × ℤ/4ℤ of Problem 2.6 in Dujella's list of open problems.

Preprint, 2026

PaperPrograms and dataLean formalization
Abstract

We show that there are infinitely many elliptic curves over ℚ with torsion subgroup ℤ/2ℤ × ℤ/4ℤ and rank at least 6, answering the case ℤ/2ℤ × ℤ/4ℤ of Problem 2.6 in Dujella's list of open problems. The largest rank previously known to occur infinitely often with this torsion was 5, due to Eroshkin. The curves are fibers of a one-parameter family of Dujella and Peral, which we show has rank exactly 3 over the rational function field. The family is a base change of an elliptic K3 surface, and quadratic twists of that surface give it three further independent points over the function field of an elliptic curve of rank one. Congruences then carry the independence of the six points, and the torsion ℤ/2ℤ × ℤ/4ℤ, from one rational point of that curve to infinitely many. The result is unconditional. It contradicts the prediction of Park, Poonen, Voight and Wood that only finitely many curves with this torsion have rank greater than 5, although the curves form a sparse family. A second family, over an elliptic curve of rank two, gives another proof.

11
Infinitely many elliptic curves with torsion ℤ/2ℤ × ℤ/6ℤ and rank at least four

We show that there are infinitely many elliptic curves over ℚ with torsion subgroup ℤ/2ℤ × ℤ/6ℤ and rank at least 4, one more than the largest rank previously known to occur infinitely often with this torsion.

Preprint, 2026

PaperPrograms and data
Abstract

We show that there are infinitely many elliptic curves over ℚ with torsion subgroup ℤ/2ℤ × ℤ/6ℤ and rank at least 4, one more than the largest rank previously known to occur infinitely often with this torsion. The universal elliptic curve with this torsion acquires four independent points over the function field of an explicit elliptic curve of rank one, and Silverman's specialisation theorem gives the family. Three of the points come from quadratic twists by polynomials that become squares on that curve, as in the rank-3 constructions of Dujella and Peral and of Dujella, Kazalicki and Peral. The fourth comes from the twist by the product of two of them; it is pulled back from a quadratic twist of a rational elliptic surface, of which the universal curve is a base change along a double cover of the line. The family is sparse, but it contradicts the prediction of Park, Poonen, Voight and Wood that only finitely many curves with this torsion have rank greater than 3.

An independent certified computation excluding four mutually unbiased bases in dimension six

We show that there are no four mutually unbiased bases in ℂ6, so that the maximum number of such bases is three, as Zauner conjectured. The proof is computer-assisted, assumes that binary64 arithmetic was carried out correctly, and carries out the discretization proof of a recent preprint by OpenAI.

Preprint, 2026

PaperPrograms and data
Abstract

We show that there are no four mutually unbiased bases in ℂ⁶, so that the maximum number of such bases is three, as Zauner conjectured. The proof is computer-assisted and assumes that the processor, compiler and C library of the computer that ran our computation carried out its binary64 (double-precision) arithmetic correctly. The computation carries out the discretization proof of a recent preprint by OpenAI, which has not appeared in a refereed journal; our programs are written from its text, and we prove every statement of the preprint that our proof uses. Four mutually unbiased bases could be taken to consist of the standard basis, the columns of a complex Hadamard matrix divided by √6, and two further bases. Finitely many balls in phase coordinates cover all complex Hadamard matrices of order six up to equivalence, and on each small ball a finite search excludes these two further bases. Every inequality that the programs decide in order to reject a case or to cover a set of matrices is decided in integer arithmetic or in interval arithmetic with outward rounding. The programs, logs and data of the computation are deposited.

Imaginary quadratic fields of class number at most 3000

Assume Kimura's explicit bound for real zeros of Dirichlet L-functions, which follows from the hypothesis that L(σ, χ) ≠ 0 for 7/8 < σ < 1 and every real primitive character χ. Then exactly 36 993 853 imaginary quadratic fields have class number at most 3000, and the largest absolute discriminant among them is 2 562 211 723.

Preprint, 2026

PaperPrograms and data
Abstract

Assume Kimura's explicit bound for real zeros of Dirichlet L-functions (the second part of Theorem 1 of his preprint, Lean-checked according to its author but not refereed), which follows from the hypothesis (H) that L(σ, χ) ≠ 0 for 7/8 < σ < 1 and every real primitive character χ. Then exactly 36 993 853 imaginary quadratic fields have class number at most 3000, and the largest absolute discriminant among them is 2 562 211 723. We also list the imaginary quadratic orders of class number at most 1095, and so the singular moduli of degree at most 1095. Kimura's bound gives h(D) ≥ 3562 for |D| ≥ e^35.0131 by Hecke's integral representation of the Dedekind zeta function, and a sieve in the manner of Watkins, the tabulation of Mosunov and Jacobson and an unconditional computation with binary quadratic forms treat smaller |D|. Hypothesis (H) follows from the generalized Riemann hypothesis and is the real case of the zero-free half-plane Re s > 7/8 announced by OpenAI in October 2026, whose Lean formalization is machine-checked but whose proof has not been refereed. Under (H) the sieve and the tabulation are not needed, h(D) > √|D|/(5 log|D|) for every imaginary quadratic field, and several classifications known only up to one possible exception become complete, among them those of the imaginary quadratic fields with class group of exponent 2, 4 or 8 and of the real quadratic fields of extended Richaud–Degert type with class number 1. Our lists agree with those of Kimura (h ≤ 2000), of Cremona and Sutherland (h ≤ 1000, under the generalized Riemann hypothesis) and of a computation posted on October 8, 2026, in the repository zeta-lab (h ≤ 1500, assuming that L(s, χ_D) ≠ 0 for Re s > 7/8), and with the counts of Garcia, Grenié, Lee and Molteni (h ≤ 2000, under the generalized Riemann hypothesis).

08
The generalized Fermat equation x11 + y11 = z5

We prove that x11 + y11 = z5 has no solution in nonzero coprime integers, and that for every prime p ≥ 11 the solutions of xp + yp = z5 in nonzero coprime integers with xy prime to 10 have xy ≡ −1 (mod 5).

Preprint, 2026

PaperPrograms and data
Abstract

We prove that x¹¹ + y¹¹ = z⁵ has no solution in nonzero coprime integers, and that for every prime p ≥ 11 the solutions of xᵖ + yᵖ = z⁵ in nonzero coprime integers with xy prime to 10 have xy ≡ −1 (mod 5). To a solution Darmon attaches a Frey abelian surface over Q(√5), a twist of the member at t = xᵖ/z⁵ of a one-parameter family. For p = 11, results of Chen and Koutsianas and of Billerey, Chen, Dieulefait and Freitas leave only the solutions with 10 | xy and 11 ∤ z. For these, t gives a point on a quotient of Darmon's modular curve X₀(𝔭), 𝔭 | 11, that is an elliptic curve of rank 1 over Q(√5), and Chabauty's method in the restriction of scalars excludes such points. For general p and xy prime to 10, Chen and Koutsianas show that the Frey surface is congruent modulo p, up to a quadratic twist, to one of four surfaces with complex multiplication (or, for p = 19, to one more modular form); we test this congruence at the prime √5. When xy ≡ ±2 (mod 5), the fields cut out by inertia there differ or, in one subcase, a Frobenius trace does. When xy ≡ 1 (mod 5), the reduction at 2 determines the twist, and Frobenius traces exclude the congruence: at an auxiliary prime, and on the special fiber over F₅ that the genus-2 curves defining these surfaces share over a totally ramified extension of Q₅(√5). For 10 | xy, a uniform bound on rational points of order p on abelian surfaces over Q(√5) with real multiplication would exclude this class for large p.

Real multiplication and the generalized Fermat equation x5 + y3 = zp

Assuming statements of a preprint of Pacetti and Villagra Torcomian, we show that for every prime p outside an explicit finite set, x5 + y3 = zp has no primitive solutions in several families, among them those with 3 ∤ xyz.

Preprint, 2026

PaperPrograms and data
Abstract

Let (X, Y, Z) be a primitive solution of x⁵ + y³ = zᵖ with p ≥ 7 prime. Pacetti and Villagra Torcomian attach to it a genus-2 Frey curve whose Jacobian Jₜ, t = −X⁵/Y³, has real multiplication by the integers of Q(√5). In a preprint they show that, if p lies outside an explicit finite set and 𝔭 is a prime above p, the mod-𝔭 representation of Jₜ is reducible, or congruent to a Hilbert newform with complex multiplication, or congruent to the Jacobian at t = −1/8 or t = 9/8, the parameters of 1⁵ + 2³ = 3² and (−3)⁵ + 6³ = (−3)³. Comparing the fields cut out by inertia at √5, we show that this last congruence forces 5 ∤ XYZ and restricts t to eight classes modulo 25, and, by comparing traces of Frobenius at an auxiliary prime, to four classes when p ≥ 17. A congruence multiplies the Weil pairings by a scalar, and by comparing this scalar at 3 and at √5 we show that the congruence cannot occur when 3 | Y and p ≡ 19, 29, 31, 41 (mod 60). Assuming statements of the preprint, we deduce that, for every prime p outside that explicit finite set, there are no solutions in several families of classes, among them those with 3 ∤ XYZ.

06
The generalized Fermat equation x3 + y3 = zn

We prove that the equations x3 + y3 = zn and x3 + y3 = 3zn have no solution in coprime nonzero integers for any n ≥ 3.

Preprint, 2026

Paper and SupplementPrograms and data
Abstract

We prove that the equations x³ + y³ = zⁿ and x³ + y³ = 3zⁿ have no solution in coprime nonzero integers for any n ≥ 3. For the first equation the open cases were prime exponents p ≡ 1 (mod 3) above 10⁹ outside a set of congruence classes treated by Chen and Siksek; in these cases the solution 1³ + 2³ = 3² blocks the modular method. We replace Kraus's Frey curve by a hypergeometric motive of rank three over K = Q(√−3), whose parameter at a solution is 3-adically close enough to a point of maximally unipotent monodromy for inertia at √−3 to act unipotently; the parameter of 1³ + 2³ = 3² is not. By a theorem of Calegari, Emerton and Gee, the mod p representation of the fiber, twisted by a cubic character, is the reduction of a member of a compatible system of minimal ramification, and we show that the member at a prime above 3 is irreducible and ordinary at √−3. Ramification and unconditional discriminant bounds show that its reductions have trivial semisimplification, and as the Galois group of the maximal pro-3 extension of K unramified outside √−3 is generated by one inertia group, the member stabilizes its ordinary line, a contradiction. For x³ + y³ = 3zⁿ the same argument also works at the primes p ≡ 2 (mod 3), which are inert in K, and Frey curves and descents treat the remaining exponents.

New exponents for the generalized Fermat equation x2 + y3 = zn

We determine the primitive solutions of x2 + y3 = zn for n = 11, 17 and 19 unconditionally; for n = 11 Freitas, Naskręcki and Stoll did so under the generalized Riemann hypothesis (GRH).

Preprint, 2026 · Some exponents assume GRH; one uses a preprint of Freitas and Stoll.

Paper and SupplementPrograms and data
Abstract

We determine the primitive solutions of x² + y³ = zⁿ for n = 11, 17 and 19 unconditionally; for n = 11 Freitas, Naskręcki and Stoll did so under the generalized Riemann hypothesis (GRH). For n = 25 we do not assume GRH but use a reduction of Freitas and Stoll that is available as a preprint, and for n = 13, 23, 26 and 29 we assume GRH. GRH enters only through the class numbers of eight fields of degree 24 and 30 and four of degree 56. The solutions are the trivial ones, with xyz = 0, and those coming from 3² − 2³ = 1. With earlier work this settles every exponent 6 ≤ n ≤ 36 except 31, under the same conditions. Following Freitas, Naskręcki and Stoll, and Freitas and Stoll for n = 25, we reduce to points over number fields on modular curves or on curves Y² = X⁵ + β. The new step is to find these points by descents that need only the class groups of their fields of definition or of small extensions, mostly along isogenies with kernel μ_ℓ, along 1 − σ for a diamond automorphism σ, and along 1 − ζ₅.

The primitive solutions of seven small generalized Fermat equations

Besides solutions with entries 0 and ±1, the only ones are (11, ±29, ±538) of x5 + y4 = 3z2 and (±895, −6202, 4199) of x4 + 3y3 + z3 = 0.

Preprint, 2026 · Table 4 of version 9 of Grechuk's list of open problems (arXiv:2404.08518).

PaperPrograms and data
Abstract

Version 9 of Grechuk's list of open Diophantine equations contains seven equations ax^p+by^q+cz^r=0 with 1/p+1/q+1/r<1 and |a|2^p+|b|2^q+|c|2^r<=60 for which the complete list of primitive solutions was not known. We determine the primitive solutions of all seven: x^5+y^4-2z^2=0, x^5+y^4-3z^2=0, x^5+y^4+3z^2=0, 2x^4-y^4+z^3=0, x^4+3y^3+2z^3=0, x^4+4y^3+z^3=0 and x^4+3y^3+z^3=0. Besides solutions with entries 0 and ±1, the only ones are (11,±29,±538) of x^5+y^4=3z^2 and (±895,-6202,4199) of x^4+3y^3+z^3=0. For the last equation this answers a question of Grechuk; J. Agbanwa has announced a proof in an unpublished manuscript, and ours is by a different method. The proofs combine descent over number fields of small degree with elliptic Chabauty and the Mordell-Weil sieve. For x^4+3y^3+z^3=0 we also use a covering by a curve of genus one over a number field of degree 12, whose Jacobian has unknown rank, and Stoll's form of Chabauty's method at the prime 2, which uses a Selmer group of a 2-isogeny in place of the Mordell-Weil group. No step assumes the generalized Riemann hypothesis. Programs and data: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23055562.

03
The generalized Fermat equation x2 + y5 = z9

We show that the equation x2 + y5 = z9 has no solution in coprime nonzero integers. No step assumes the generalized Riemann hypothesis.

Preprint, 2026

PaperPrograms and data
Abstract

We show that the equation x^2+y^5=z^9 has no solution in coprime nonzero integers. Equivalently, none of the equations x^2+y^5=z^9, x^2+y^9=z^5 and x^5+y^9=z^2 has such a solution. By Edwards' parametrization of the coprime solutions of a^2+b^3+c^5=0, a solution gives coprime integers u, v at which one of 27 binary forms g_i of degree 20 takes the value -z^3. A 2-adic argument and a descent modulo cubes over number fields of degree at most 10 exclude 22 of the 27 forms and, for each of the other five, determine the values of the factors of g_i up to cubes. These five forms lead to rational points on an elliptic curve over Q, on a curve of genus 2 over Q, and on elliptic curves over Q(5^(1/3)) and over Q(sqrt(-5)). We determine the relevant points by a 2-descent over Q, by elliptic Chabauty over a cubic field together with a Mordell-Weil sieve, by a descent via 3-isogeny over Q(5^(1/3)), and by elliptic Chabauty over Q(sqrt(-5)). No step assumes the generalized Riemann hypothesis. Programs and data: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23004600.

02
The generalized Fermat equation x2 + y5 = z7

We show that the equation x2 + y5 = z7 has no solution in nonzero coprime integers, assuming a classification theorem from Putz's thesis, which has not appeared in a refereed journal.

Preprint, 2026

PaperPrograms and dataLean formalization
Abstract

We show that the equation x² + y⁵ = z⁷ has no solution in nonzero coprime integers, assuming a classification theorem from Putz's thesis, which has not appeared in a refereed journal. Equivalently, none of the equations x² + y⁵ = z⁷, x² + y⁷ = z⁵, x⁵ + y⁷ = z² has a solution in nonzero coprime integers. Putz attaches to a solution an octic algebra, the fibre of a Belyi map of degree 8, and proves that it is always isomorphic to one fixed octic field L₈. We prove unconditionally that no solution has fibre isomorphic to L₈, by a fifth-power descent over a field L₂₄ of degree 24 that Putz introduced, completed by fifth-power residue symbols at five auxiliary primes. We also prove, without assuming the generalized Riemann hypothesis, that L₂₄ has class number 1, using a criterion of Belabas, Diaz y Diaz and Friedman.

01
The generalized Fermat equation X5 + Y3 = Z7

Assuming the classification of Dahmen–Siksek and Putz, we show that X5 + Y3 = Z7 has no solution in nonzero coprime integers.

Preprint, 2026 Submitted

PaperPrograms and data
Abstract

Assuming the classification of Dahmen–Siksek and Putz, we show that X^5+Y^3=Z^7 has no solution in nonzero coprime integers; equivalently, the generalized Fermat equation of signature (3,5,7) has no nontrivial primitive solution in any arrangement of the exponents. By that classification, a solution determines one of seven septic fields: six pure fields Q((3^a·5)^(1/7)), 1 ≤ a ≤ 6, and an exceptional field F. We exclude F by a fifth-power descent over F(√−35), where the equation becomes a norm identity for an explicit element; the identity and the element's valuations above 3, 5 and 7 confine it to 125 classes modulo fifth powers, none compatible with its possible fifth-power residue symbols above 11 and 19. For the pure fields we use results from recent preprints on the genus-2 Frey curves of Pacetti and Villagra Torcomian, whose Jacobians have real multiplication by Q(√5). Level lowering shows that their 7-torsion is reducible, giving a rational point on a genus-15 double cover of the genus-8 curve of F_49-lines in the 7-torsion. The Prym variety of this cover contains an abelian surface of rank one, and Chabauty's method at 13 confines the point to two explicit rational points, neither coming from a solution. Supplementary data and programs: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22949370. P. Chocian (arXiv:2609.26996) has proved the same theorem independently, by different methods.

FAQ

Why AI for mathematics?

I've been interested in both for a long time, so putting them together was natural. I ended up focusing on generalized Fermat equations because I made a lot of progress there while working on a larger problem, but I still work in other areas too.

What is your part?

I work on the system itself, mostly research and experiments, and I choose the problems and set the priorities. Sometimes I weigh in on mathematical strategy. The system is built from standard multi-agent techniques. Most of the effort goes into filling their gaps and adapting them to mathematics.

Which AI models do you use?

The latest frontier models, at their highest reasoning settings when it helps. Mostly Claude Code or Codex, and sometimes both, since different models catch different mistakes and come up with different ideas.

How are the results checked?

Several times, in different ways. Some checks go through a whole proof, others a single step. Not all of them are independent, and some try to break a result instead of confirming it. Computations get the same treatment, and some main theorems are also formalized in Lean. When I can, I also get outside checks, so I know how well things are working. Let me know if you find a problem.

How do you decide what to post?

I only post results that have been checked carefully and that I believe are new and useful. Usually that means answering a question people have actually asked, or giving others a method or computation they can use.

Is the writing final?

Not yet. It's one of the main things I'm working on. The newest few papers are in a good spot, and the earlier ones are still being improved.

Do you collaborate? Can I ask you something?

Sure, email me.
