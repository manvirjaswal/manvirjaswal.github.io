An effective André–Oort theorem for Shimura varieties

We give effective bounds for the special subvarieties that are maximal in a subvariety of a Shimura variety. The bounds on the field and the level assume a zero-free half-plane, which a preprint of Cureton and Thorner, formalized in Lean, establishes.

SubmittedPaperPrograms and data
Abstract

We give effective bounds for the special subvarieties V that are maximal in a subvariety Z of a Shimura variety, where Z is geometrically irreducible and defined over a number field and the level is neat and of product form: for the degree of V, with no hypothesis; and for the discriminant of a number field L_V attached to V and the Klingler–Yafaev invariant α_V of the level, if for some δ > 0 the Dedekind zeta function of L_V has no zero with real part greater than 1 − δ. The bound for the degree depends only on the degree of Z, and the other two on the degree of Z, the degree of its field of definition and δ; all three are effectively computable, given representatives of the connected components of the adjoint Shimura variety. Cureton and Thorner prove that such a half-plane exists with δ = 1/32 for every number field; with their theorem, all these bounds hold with no further hypothesis. For abelian type, an algorithm lists these subvarieties from the bounds, given a finite étale cover by a Shimura variety of Hodge type inside one of Mumford's moduli spaces and equations for the preimage of Z.

Diophantine definitions of ℤ in totally real fields from rank-one elliptic curves

We prove that ℤ is Diophantine in a totally real field K, with 25 unknowns, if one of four explicit elliptic curves E: y2 = x3 − Ax over a real quadratic field F ⊈ K has rank one over L = KF, with torsion in E[2] and 2E(L) ⊆ E(F). For a suitable curve these conditions hold for every layer of the cyclotomic ℤ2-extension of ℚ and for infinitely many real quadratic fields.

PaperPrograms and data
Abstract

Let K be a totally real number field, let E: y² = x³ − Ax be one of four explicit elliptic curves over a real quadratic field F ⊈ K, and put L = KF. We prove that if E(L) has rank one and torsion in E[2], and 2E(L) ⊆ E(F), then ℤ is Diophantine in K, with 25 unknowns, so Hilbert's tenth problem over K has a negative answer. For a suitable curve these conditions hold for every layer of the cyclotomic ℤ₂-extension of ℚ, for infinitely many real quadratic fields and for some further fields of degree at most 8. The construction is Z.-W. Sun's, from his proofs for ℚ(√2), ℚ(√3) and ℚ(√6), except that our predicate excluding poles of odd order uses the congruent-number twists of our earlier proof for ℚ. Its main step is a height bound on a q-adic tube of a quaternionic Shimura curve. We prove it over every totally real field by Sun's method, with a scalar cochain over K, a comparison with Shimura curves over K in every degree and, when √3 ∈ K, a second tube prime.

Orbit closures of generic rational polygons with at least five sides

Mirzakhani and Wright ask whether, for more than four sides, the orbit closure of the unfolding of almost every rational polygon with given angles can be anything other than a connected component of a stratum of Abelian differentials or a locus of double covers of a component of a stratum of quadratic differentials. It cannot.

PaperPrograms and data
Abstract

Mirzakhani and Wright attach to the angles θ of a rational polygon the orbit closure ℳ(θ) of the unfolding of almost every polygon with these angles. They ask whether, for more than four sides, ℳ(θ) can be anything other than a connected component of a stratum of Abelian differentials or a locus of double covers of a component of a stratum of quadratic differentials. It cannot. For every choice of five or more angles, ℳ(θ) is the component of its stratum if the least common denominator k of the angles divided by π is odd, and the locus of holonomy double covers of the component of its quadratic stratum if k is even. When some s of the angles sum to (s − 1)π, Apisa and Aygun treat this case by a different method; our proof does not depend on theirs. We read the rank of ℳ(θ) off characters of the deck rotation of the unfolding and induct on the number of sides by colliding two vertices; where no collision applies, arithmetic modulo k takes its place. We also extend Mirzakhani and Wright's nonvanishing of the second fundamental form from triangles to every number of sides.

Orbit closures of scalene rational triangles: every denominator

Delecroix and Rüth conjectured that, apart from an explicit list, the unfoldings of rational triangles and quadrilaterals without a reflection symmetry have dense GL(2,ℝ)-orbits in their ambient strata, and proved this for the triangles with angles piπ/N and N ≤ 58. We prove the conjecture for triangles.

Paper and SupplementPrograms and data
Abstract

Delecroix and Rüth conjectured that, apart from an explicit list, the unfoldings of rational triangles and quadrilaterals without a reflection symmetry have dense GL(2,ℝ)-orbits in their ambient strata, and proved this for the triangles with angles pᵢπ/N and N ≤ 58. We prove the conjecture for triangles. If a scalene rational triangle is not on their list of three infinite families and nine triangles, the orbit closure of its unfolding is the connected component of its stratum when N is odd, and the locus of holonomy double covers of a connected component of a stratum of quadratic differentials when N is even. Most of the proof concerns subgroups of the units modulo N; parts of it, including all of N ≤ 58, are computer-assisted.

Obtuse rational lattice triangles

Answering a question of Hooper, we prove that an obtuse rational triangle is a lattice triangle if and only if its angles are (π/n, π/n, (n − 2)π/n) with n ≥ 5, (π/2k, π/k, (2k − 3)π/2k) with k ≥ 4, or (π/12, π/3, 7π/12).

SubmittedPaperPrograms and data
Abstract

A rational triangle is a lattice triangle if the Veech group of its unfolding is a lattice. Answering a question of Hooper, we prove that an obtuse rational triangle is a lattice triangle if and only if its angles are (π/n, π/n, (n − 2)π/n) with n ≥ 5, (π/2k, π/k, (2k − 3)π/2k) with k ≥ 4, or (π/12, π/3, 7π/12). The idea is a first-order test on relative periods. By Möller's theorem, on a Veech surface the difference of two zeros of the holomorphic form is torsion in the factor of the Jacobian with real multiplication, and stays torsion along the Teichmüller curve. So certain relative periods vanish along the Teichmüller disc, and on a triangle unfolding their first derivative is an integral over the plane that can be computed in closed form. For obtuse angle at most 2π/3 we determine for every denominator where the criterion of Mirzakhani and Wright is blind, and exclude all but four of the triangles there by relative periods and by cylinders whose moduli or areas cannot occur on a Veech surface. For obtuse angle above 2π/3 the proof uses a theorem of Larsen, Norton and Zykoski. Parts of the proof are computer-assisted.

The rational points of the non-split Cartan modular curve of level 121

We prove that the modular curve Xns+(121), of genus 511, has exactly seven rational points, all of them CM. This is the level 121 case of a conjecture of Rouse, Sutherland and Zureick-Brown, and with their theorem it determines the 11-adic images of Galois of non-CM elliptic curves over ℚ.

PaperPrograms and data
Abstract

We prove that the modular curve X⁺ₙₛ(121) of the normalizer of a non-split Cartan subgroup of level 121, of genus 511, has exactly seven rational points, all of them CM. This is the level 121 case of a conjecture of Rouse, Sutherland and Zureick-Brown, and with their theorem it determines the 11-adic images of Galois of non-CM elliptic curves over ℚ. The Jacobian has rank equal to the genus. At p = 17 and 43 one Hecke block of the Mordell–Weil group has logarithm divisible by p² but p-adic height of valuation one, and a comparison of valuations in the quadratic Chabauty identity then shows that each CM point is the only rational point in its residue disc, with no computation of the local height at p, no basis of the Mordell–Weil group and no equations for the curve. The inputs come from expansions of differentials at CM points and from a formula we prove for intersection numbers of CM divisors of coprime fundamental discriminants under Hecke operators on X⁺ₙₛ(ℓᵉ) (Theorem 1.3).

Infinitely many elliptic curves with torsion ℤ/2ℤ × ℤ/4ℤ and rank at least six

We show that there are infinitely many elliptic curves over ℚ with torsion subgroup ℤ/2ℤ × ℤ/4ℤ and rank at least 6. This answers the case ℤ/2ℤ × ℤ/4ℤ of Problem 2.6 in Dujella's list of open problems.

PaperPrograms and dataLean formalization
Abstract

We show that there are infinitely many elliptic curves over ℚ with torsion subgroup ℤ/2ℤ × ℤ/4ℤ and rank at least 6, answering the case ℤ/2ℤ × ℤ/4ℤ of Problem 2.6 in Dujella's list of open problems. The largest rank previously known to occur infinitely often with this torsion was 5, due to Eroshkin. The curves are fibers of a one-parameter family of Dujella and Peral, which we show has rank exactly 3 over the rational function field. The family is a base change of an elliptic K3 surface, and quadratic twists of that surface give it three further independent points over the function field of an elliptic curve of rank one. Congruences then carry the independence of the six points, and the torsion ℤ/2ℤ × ℤ/4ℤ, from one rational point of that curve to infinitely many. The result is unconditional. It contradicts the prediction of Park, Poonen, Voight and Wood that only finitely many curves with this torsion have rank greater than 5, although the curves form a sparse family. A second family, over an elliptic curve of rank two, gives another proof.

The generalized Fermat equation x3 + y3 = zn

We prove that the equations x3 + y3 = zn and x3 + y3 = 3zn have no solution in coprime nonzero integers for any n ≥ 3.

Paper and SupplementPrograms and data
Abstract

We prove that the equations x³ + y³ = zⁿ and x³ + y³ = 3zⁿ have no solution in coprime nonzero integers for any n ≥ 3. For the first equation the open cases were prime exponents p ≡ 1 (mod 3) above 10⁹ outside a set of congruence classes treated by Chen and Siksek; in these cases the solution 1³ + 2³ = 3² blocks the modular method. We replace Kraus's Frey curve by a hypergeometric motive of rank three over K = Q(√−3), whose parameter at a solution is 3-adically close enough to a point of maximally unipotent monodromy for inertia at √−3 to act unipotently; the parameter of 1³ + 2³ = 3² is not. By a theorem of Calegari, Emerton and Gee, the mod p representation of the fiber, twisted by a cubic character, is the reduction of a member of a compatible system of minimal ramification, and we show that the member at a prime above 3 is irreducible and ordinary at √−3. Ramification and unconditional discriminant bounds show that its reductions have trivial semisimplification, and as the Galois group of the maximal pro-3 extension of K unramified outside √−3 is generated by one inertia group, the member stabilizes its ordinary line, a contradiction. For x³ + y³ = 3zⁿ the same argument also works at the primes p ≡ 2 (mod 3), which are inert in K, and Frey curves and descents treat the remaining exponents.

Orbit closures of generic rational quadrilaterals

Delecroix and Rüth conjectured that their lists of admissible and reduced rational triangle and quadrilateral types whose unfoldings do not have dense orbits in their ambient strata are complete, and they proved this for quadrilaterals with angles 2πai/N when N ≤ 32. We prove the conjecture for quadrilaterals, for the generic quadrilateral with given angles.

PaperPrograms and data
Abstract

Delecroix and Rüth conjectured that their lists of admissible and reduced rational triangle and quadrilateral types whose unfoldings do not have dense orbits in their ambient strata are complete, and they proved this for quadrilaterals with angles 2πaᵢ/N when N ≤ 32. We prove the conjecture for quadrilaterals, for the generic quadrilateral with given angles. For every admissible and reduced type outside their seven quadrilateral exceptions, the orbit closure of the unfolding of the generic member is a connected component of a stratum of Abelian differentials if N is odd or N ≡ 2 (mod 4), and the locus of holonomy double covers of a component of a stratum of quadratic differentials if 4 divides N. The proof reads the rank of the orbit closure off a set of characters of the deck rotation of the unfolding, a cyclic cover of the sphere. Mirzakhani and Wright's rules, as extended for triangles in the two earlier papers of this series, constrain this set, and two further inputs bring in the orbit closures of triangles: the collision of two vertices, through Mirzakhani and Wright's boundary theorem, and symmetric members of the family of cyclic covers, which cover unfoldings of triangles. Parts of the proof, including a census up to N = 1000, are computer-assisted.

Density of blow-up data and regularity at the blow-up time for the three-dimensional Euler equations

Alpöge, Buckmaster and Coiculescu have constructed smooth odd initial data on 𝕋3 whose solutions of the incompressible Euler equations blow up in finite time. Running their construction without oddness, we show that data whose solutions blow up in arbitrarily short time are dense among smooth divergence-free fields in L∞, H1 and Cα, 0 < α < 5/6.

Paper
Abstract

Alpöge, Buckmaster and Coiculescu have constructed smooth odd initial data on 𝕋³ whose solutions of the incompressible Euler equations blow up in finite time. Running their construction without oddness, with the singularity carried by a fluid particle, we show that data whose solutions blow up in arbitrarily short time are dense among smooth divergence-free fields in L^∞, H¹ and C^α, 0 < α < 5/6. The same holds inside classes of fields invariant under a finite group of isometries of 𝕋³, such as the Taylor–Green and Kida classes, with blow-up at the same time along a whole orbit. The Euler equations are not well posed in these topologies, and the result says nothing about instability in C^{1,α} or in Hˢ, s > 5/2. For both our solutions and those of Alpöge, Buckmaster and Coiculescu, the velocity stays continuous up to the blow-up time T in H¹ and in C^α for every 0 < α < 5/6, so the enstrophy converges, while u(T) ∉ C^{5/6}, curl u(T) lies in no Lᵖ with p > 2, and the vorticity on the particle grows faster than (T − t)⁻².

Orbit closures of scalene rational triangles: rank one and prime denominators

We determine the scalene rational triangles of rank one: besides the known lattice triangles, they are the two triangles found by Delecroix and Rüth's computer search, with angles (π/10, 3π/10, 3π/5) and (π/12, π/4, 2π/3). We also prove that for a scalene triangle with angles piπ/N, N prime, the orbit closure of the unfolding is a connected component of its stratum.

PaperPrograms and data
Abstract

The unfolding of a rational triangle is a translation surface, and the closure of its GL(2,ℝ)-orbit has a rank, which is one for lattice surfaces. We determine the scalene rational triangles of rank one: besides the known lattice triangles, which form three infinite families and four further triangles, they are the two triangles found by Delecroix and Rüth's computer search, with angles (π/10, 3π/10, 3π/5) and (π/12, π/4, 2π/3), whose unfoldings are Prym eigenforms. A proof that ℤ is Diophantine in ℚ through congruent-number twists

We give a proof of Glazer and collaborators' theorem that ℤ is Diophantine in ℚ, which builds on OpenAI's proof that Hilbert's tenth problem over ℚ has a negative answer. Where they use a restricted 2-converse theorem, this rests on published theorems.

SubmittedPaperPrograms and data
Abstract

We give a proof of Glazer and collaborators' theorem that ℤ is Diophantine in ℚ, which builds on OpenAI's proof that Hilbert's tenth problem over ℚ has a negative answer. Integers are represented by multiples of a point on an elliptic curve of rank one over ℚ(√2). An existential condition on two congruent-number twists forbids poles of odd order at odd primes. A height bound at six rational points, from a quaternionic Shimura curve and Serre's conjecture (a theorem of Khare–Wintenberger and Kisin), and a prime pattern from the Green–Tao–Ziegler theorem turn congruences at chosen primes into identities between multiples, and the growth of a Lucas sequence forces integrality. Our contribution is the existence of the rational points that the condition on twists requires: for coprime squarefree integers β > 1 and θ ≥ 1 with β ≡ θ ≡ 1 (mod 8), each a square modulo the primes of the other, there is an integer e such that y² = x³ − e²x and y² = x³ − e²θ²x both have rank one and a rational point with abscissa in β·(ℚ×)². Where Glazer and collaborators use a restricted 2-converse theorem, this rests on published theorems: the genus criterion of Tian, Yuan and Zhang, with Gross–Zagier.

The rational points of the non-split Cartan modular curve of level 25

We prove that the modular curve Xns+(25), of genus 14 and Jacobian rank 14, has exactly nine rational points, all of them CM. This is the level 25 case of a conjecture of Rouse, Sutherland and Zureick-Brown, and with their theorem it determines the 5-adic images of Galois of elliptic curves over ℚ without complex multiplication.

PaperPrograms and data
Abstract

We prove that the modular curve X⁺ₙₛ(25) of the normalizer of a non-split Cartan subgroup of level 25, of genus 14 and Jacobian rank 14, has exactly nine rational points, all of them CM. This is the level 25 case of a conjecture of Rouse, Sutherland and Zureick-Brown, and with their theorem it determines the 5-adic images of Galois of elliptic curves over ℚ without complex multiplication. A descent by the modular units of the curve, using no information on the Mordell–Weil group, places every rational point in the residue disc of a CM point at the prime 1151. Triplets of mutually unbiased bases in dimension six

We prove that every triplet of mutually unbiased bases of ℂ6 is equivalent to a triplet from Zauner's construction. This is Conjecture 1 of Matolcsi, Matszangosz, Varga and Weiner. The proof is computer-assisted and assumes stated hypotheses on the computations.

PaperPrograms and data
Abstract

We prove that every triplet of mutually unbiased bases of ℂ⁶ is equivalent, by a unitary map and a relabeling of the bases and of their vectors, up to phases, to a triplet from Zauner's construction. This is Conjecture 1 of Matolcsi, Matszangosz, Varga and Weiner, which we call the Budapest conjecture: up to equivalence, each such triplet lies in exactly one of two cases, the two-parameter family described by Szöllősi or one isolated triplet. The four authors' Conjecture 2, which they regard as the main conjecture of their paper, holds as well. Cárdenes Wuttig and Tindall have posted a different computer-assisted proof of the Budapest conjecture. We show without a computer that a triplet comes from the construction exactly when it has a unitary symmetry of order three up to a scalar that fixes every vector of one basis up to a phase and permutes the vectors of the other two up to phases; we call such a symmetry a clock, as Sun does for the Fourier family. To show that every triplet has a clock, we follow it through a finite cover of the complex Hadamard matrices of order six up to equivalence by certified balls, along an acyclic directed graph, to a ball near the Fourier family, where its clock is certified. In addition, we determine which pairs of bases lie in a triplet, and we recover that there are no four mutually unbiased bases in ℂ⁶. The proof is computer-assisted and assumes stated hypotheses on the computations (Appendix A).

The generalized Fermat equation xp + yp = z5 and relative class numbers

We prove that the equation xp + yp = z5 has no solution in nonzero coprime integers for every prime p ≥ 13 such that p ≢ 1 (mod 5) and 5 ∤ h−(ℚ(ζ5p)), using results of Chen and Koutsianas (to appear in Algebra & Number Theory) on Darmon's Frey surfaces. These conditions hold at 41 primes 13 ≤ p < 1000 and at 2432 primes 13 ≤ p < 105.

PaperPrograms and data
Abstract

We prove that the equation xᵖ + yᵖ = z⁵ has no solution in nonzero coprime integers for every prime p ≥ 13 such that p ≢ 1 (mod 5) and 5 ∤ h⁻(ℚ(ζ₅ₚ)), using results of Chen and Koutsianas (to appear in Algebra & Number Theory) on Darmon's Frey surfaces. These conditions hold at 41 primes 13 ≤ p < 1000 and at 2432 primes 13 ≤ p < 10⁵. For every prime p ≥ 7 with p ≢ 1 (mod 5) and 5 ∤ h⁻(ℚ(ζ₅ₚ)) we prove, without these results, that no solution (a, b, c) has 10p ∣ ab. Such solutions are close, at each of 2, 5 and p, to one of the trivial solutions (1, 0, 1), (0, 1, 1), which survive every local test. To exclude such solutions we attach to a solution a curve of genus 2(p − 1) whose Jacobian has multiplication by ℤ[ζ₅, ζₚ + ζₚ⁻¹]. It belongs to a family of quotients, by an involution, of cyclic covers in a hypergeometric family of the kind used in Darmon's program. The member of this family at the trivial solution is Y⁵ = Rᵖ − 1, which has complex multiplication, and we compare the 5-adic Tate modules of the two curves one power of 1 − ζ₅ at a time. The obstructions to extending this comparison lie in Selmer groups that vanish when 5 ∤ h⁻(ℚ(ζ₅ₚ)), as in Kummer's theorem for regular primes; the local condition at 5 comes from Breuil–Kisin modules over a tamely ramified extension. The generalized Riemann hypothesis is not used.

Elliptic curves with torsion group ℤ/6ℤ and generic rank four

We give an elliptic curve over ℚ(t) with non-constant j-invariant, torsion subgroup ℤ/6ℤ and Mordell–Weil rank exactly 4. The largest rank previously known for such a curve was 3, due to Lecacheux and reached by several later families.

PaperPrograms and data
Abstract

We give an elliptic curve over ℚ(t) with non-constant j-invariant, torsion subgroup ℤ/6ℤ and Mordell–Weil rank exactly 4. The largest rank previously known for such a curve was 3, due to Lecacheux and reached by several later families. The curve is the pullback of the universal elliptic curve with a point of order 6 along an explicit rational function of degree 16 that is invariant under t ↦ −t and t ↦ 1/t. The upper bound for the rank comes from the specialization criterion of Gusić and Tadić and a 2-descent of a single fiber. The rank splits along the characters of the symmetry group: the curve descends to ℚ((t + 1/t)²), where it and three of its quadratic twists have rank exactly 1. All but finitely many rational fibers have torsion subgroup ℤ/6ℤ and rank at least 4. We also give an explicit family over an elliptic curve of rank two which shows that infinitely many elliptic curves over ℚ have torsion subgroup ℤ/6ℤ and rank at least 5, a bound that Dujella's tables credit to Eroshkin (2009, personal communication). One member of this family has rank exactly 6. Over ℚ(√2) the same kind of construction gives infinitely many curves with torsion subgroup ℤ/6ℤ and rank at least 6; whether the same holds over ℚ is open. All results are unconditional.

PaperPrograms and data
Abstract

We show that there are infinitely many elliptic curves over ℚ with torsion subgroup ℤ/2ℤ × ℤ/6ℤ and rank at least 4, one more than the largest rank previously known to occur infinitely often with this torsion. The universal elliptic curve with this torsion acquires four independent points over the function field of an explicit elliptic curve of rank one, and Silverman's specialisation theorem gives the family. An independent certified computation excluding four mutually unbiased bases in dimension six

We show that there are no four mutually unbiased bases in ℂ6, so that the maximum number of such bases is three, as Zauner conjectured. The proof is computer-assisted, assumes that binary64 arithmetic was carried out correctly, and carries out the discretization proof of a recent preprint by OpenAI.

PaperPrograms and data
Abstract

We show that there are no four mutually unbiased bases in ℂ⁶, so that the maximum number of such bases is three, as Zauner conjectured. The proof is computer-assisted and assumes that the processor, compiler and C library of the computer that ran our computation carried out its binary64 (double-precision) arithmetic correctly. The computation carries out the discretization proof of a recent preprint by OpenAI, which has not appeared in a refereed journal; our programs are written from its text, and we prove every statement of the preprint that our proof uses. Four mutually unbiased bases could be taken to consist of the standard basis, the columns of a complex Hadamard matrix divided by √6, and two further bases. Finitely many balls in phase coordinates cover all complex Hadamard matrices of order six up to equivalence, and on each small ball a finite search excludes these two further bases. Every inequality that the programs decide in order to reject a case or to cover a set of matrices is decided in integer arithmetic or in interval arithmetic with outward rounding. The programs, logs and data of the computation are deposited.
